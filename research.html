01✳

Cost closes doors.

In NORC’s 2022 survey, 75% of U.S. adults believed people do not attend college because they cannot afford it.

NORC at the University of Chicago · May 3, 2022 ↗

02✳

A four-year price tag.

Education Data Initiative reports an average of $39,406 per year for first-time, full-time undergraduates at four-year institutions, including books, supplies, and daily living expenses.

$39,406× 4 years =$157,624

A simple four-year illustration at the same annual rate. Actual costs vary by school, aid, living arrangements, and time to graduate.

Education Data Initiative · updated September 5, 2026 ↗

03✳

Research funding matters.

A July 2025 Center for American Progress analysis of federal data identified grants targeted for termination with a total award value of $6.9 billion using USAspending data.

About $3.3 billion remained unspent on those awards. The full award value is not the same as money still available to be cut. This is a dated analysis, not a current total.

Center for American Progress · July 23, 2025 ↗

04✳

Budgets become opportunities.

Reduced funding can put research, programs, and student opportunities at risk. Our concern: schools may face pressure to reduce services or seek other revenue, including tuition.

Higher costs can make borrowing more likely. Debt is not inevitable for every student—and cost should not decide who gets to learn.
